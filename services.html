The Method

Map. Build. Hand Off.

The sprint is short enough to keep momentum and concrete enough to leave you with a system, not a recommendation deck.

1

Map

Week 1

I inventory the tools already in use, trace the manual handoffs, and separate the steps where AI helps from the ones where it does not. Then you and I choose one workflow and write down the finish line.

Output: current-state map, sprint scope, owner, safeguards, and success criteria.

2

Build

Weeks 2–3

I wire the systems together, add the AI-assisted steps, and keep people in the loop wherever judgment, risk, or client contact lives. The workflow is tested on representative work against the criteria from week one.

Output: one production-ready automation running in accounts you control.

3

Hand Off

Finish line

I train the operator, document how the workflow runs and how to handle exceptions, and make sure the credentials and ownership sit with your business. I also rank the next two or three sensible automations.

Output: runbook, owner checklist, and prioritized backlog of the next two or three automations.

What Actually Gets Built

Illustrative workflows that can fit a professional services firm or owner-run operation. The sprint still starts with your tools and your actual bottleneck.

Intake & Onboarding

  • Client intake orchestrationOne intake form feeds every downstream system: matter or engagement setup, folders, conflict or screening checks queued, kickoff scheduled. No re-keying.
  • Engagement letters & proposalsFirst drafts generated from intake data and your own precedents. Partners review and sign instead of assembling from scratch.
  • Document collection chasingClients get a checklist and polite automated reminders until everything arrives. Your staff stops playing fetch.
  • New-client screeningInbound inquiries checked against your fit criteria and flagged for review, with a drafted response either way.

Documents & Drafting

  • Document intake & filingInbound documents read, classified, named by your conventions, and filed to the right matter or engagement, with exceptions queued for a person.
  • Review prep summariesLong documents summarized against what you actually need to check, so review starts at page one with a map.
  • First-draft generationRoutine drafting grounded in your firm’s own templates and precedents, never a public chatbot’s guess.
  • Version & deadline trackingKey dates extracted on arrival and tracked automatically, with reminders that escalate.

Billing & Collections

  • Time & WIP captureWork activity turned into draft time entries and WIP visibility, so billing stops depending on Friday memory.
  • Invoice draftingBills assembled from captured time and firm rates, then queued for partner approval instead of built from scratch.
  • Collections follow-upOverdue invoices chased with polite, escalating sequences in your voice. Partners only handle the conversations that need them.
  • Trust & retainer alertsBalances monitored with replenishment requests drafted before work outruns the retainer.

Client Communication

  • Client status reporting“Where do things stand?” answered before it’s asked: status updates drafted from matter activity on a schedule you set.
  • Email triageThe shared inbox classified, routed, and answered in draft, grounded in your business’s knowledge. People handle the messages that need judgment.
  • Scheduling & follow-upsMeeting requests, confirmations, reschedules, and post-meeting follow-up notes handled without inbox ping-pong.
  • Meeting prep briefsClient history, open items, and recent activity compiled before every meeting, so nobody walks in cold.

FAQ

Fair Questions, Straight Answers

How do you handle our client data? Some of it is privileged or confidential.+

Head-on, in writing, before anything is built. Every engagement includes a data handling appendix that states exactly what data each automation touches, where it is processed, what leaves your systems (often nothing), and which AI providers see it under what terms, including a written commitment that your data never trains their models. Where the work is sensitive, builds run inside your own accounts and tenants rather than mine. I spent years walking enterprise security teams through exactly these reviews. If your malpractice carrier or bar rules impose requirements, I design to them, and if a workflow can’t be done safely within them, I’ll say so during the map phase instead of finding out later.

What happens if the build misses its success criteria?+

You keep everything: the working build as far as it got, all documentation, and a written analysis of why it missed. No automatic extension and no open-ended rebuild. The criteria exist so this moment is a decision, not an argument. If I don’t believe the workflow can pass, I’ll say so during the map phase and redirect the sprint before the build starts.

Who owns the automations afterward?+

Your business does. Everything runs in accounts you control, with documentation, credentials, a runbook, and an owner checklist handed over as part of the engagement. There is no proprietary platform you rent from me and no dependency designed to keep you paying. If you never call me again, your team has what it needs to run the workflow. That’s deliberate: Advisory exists for businesses that want ongoing help, not as a hostage arrangement.

Which tools do you build with?+

Whatever the problem actually needs, which is a real answer, not a dodge. Usually that means connecting the tools you already run (practice management, document management, email, accounting) with workflow platforms like Zapier, Make, or n8n, plus AI models for the reading, drafting, and summarizing steps. Sometimes it’s custom code against your systems’ APIs. I’m not a reseller for any platform, so the recommendation is never shaped by a commission.

Do we need clean data or documented processes first?+

No. Most businesses do not, and waiting for perfect documentation is how automation never starts. The map phase works from how things actually run, including the messy parts and the process that lives in your office manager’s head. Clean documentation is an output of this work, not a prerequisite for it.

Is this going to replace our staff?+

In practice it replaces the worst parts of their jobs: the re-keying, the chasing, the assembling. The businesses I work with are trying to grow without every new customer adding admin overhead. A paralegal, practice manager, or operations lead usually becomes the automation’s operator, not its casualty.

Why isn’t pricing posted on this page?+

Because a fair price depends on your tools, your process, and how much needs to change to hit the success criteria you and I agree on. I’d rather scope it honestly on a call than post a range that’s wrong for your business either way. You’ll have an exact number in writing before anything starts, and the intro call is free.

You only take one engagement at a time. What if you're busy?+

Then you get a start date, in writing, before you commit to anything. Advisory work can run alongside an active build; AI Workflow Sprints queue one at a time. The tradeoff is real and deliberate: you get a senior operator’s full attention and a finish line, instead of an agency’s junior bench and an open-ended timeline.
