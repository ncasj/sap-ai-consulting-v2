FAQ

Fair Questions, Straight Answers

How do you handle our client data? Some of it is privileged or confidential.+

Head-on, in writing, before anything is built. Every engagement includes a data handling appendix that states exactly what data each automation touches, where it is processed, what leaves your systems (often nothing), and which AI providers see it under what terms, including a written commitment that your data never trains their models. Where the work is sensitive, builds run inside your own accounts and tenants rather than mine. I spent years walking enterprise security teams through exactly these reviews. If your malpractice carrier or bar rules impose requirements, we design to them, and if a workflow can’t be done safely within them, I’ll say so at the audit stage instead of finding out later.

What happens if the build misses its success criteria?+

You stop, and you keep everything: the working build as far as it got, all documentation, and a written analysis of why it missed. No renegotiation, no “iteration is part of the journey.” The criteria exist precisely so this moment is a decision, not an argument. And if I don’t believe a build can pass, I’ll tell you at the audit stage and we won’t start one.

Who owns the automations afterward?+

Your firm does. Everything runs in accounts you control, with documentation, credentials, and runbooks handed over as part of the engagement. There is no proprietary platform you rent from me and no dependency designed to keep you paying. If you never call me again, everything keeps working and your team knows how to run it. That’s deliberate: the Advisory tier exists for firms that want ongoing help, not as a hostage arrangement.

Which tools do you build with?+

Whatever the problem actually needs, which is a real answer, not a dodge. Usually that means connecting the tools you already run (practice management, document management, email, accounting) with workflow platforms like Zapier, Make, or n8n, plus AI models for the reading, drafting, and summarizing steps. Sometimes it’s custom code against your systems’ APIs. I’m not a reseller for any platform, so the recommendation is never shaped by a commission.

Do we need clean data or documented processes first?+

No. Almost no firm has them, and waiting for them is how automation never starts. The audit works from how things actually run, including the messy parts and the process that lives in your office manager’s head. Clean documentation is an output of this work, not a prerequisite for it.

Is this going to replace our staff?+

In practice it replaces the worst parts of their jobs: the re-keying, the chasing, the assembling. Firms I work with aren’t trying to shrink. They’re trying to grow without every new client adding admin overhead, and to point their best people at billable, judgment heavy work. Your paralegal or practice manager usually becomes the automation’s operator, not its casualty.

Why isn’t pricing posted on this page?+

Because a fair price depends on your tools, your process, and how much needs to change to hit the success criteria we agree on. I’d rather scope it honestly on a call than post a range that’s wrong for your firm either way. You’ll have an exact number in writing before anything starts, and the intro call is free.

You only take one engagement at a time. What if you're busy?+

Then you get a start date, in writing, before you commit to anything. Advisory work can run alongside an active build; Audit + Implementation engagements queue one at a time. The tradeoff is real and I’ve chosen it deliberately: you get a senior operator’s full attention and a finish line, instead of an agency’s junior bench and an open-ended timeline.
