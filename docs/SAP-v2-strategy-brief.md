## 2. Positioning and narrative

**Positioning statement (working draft for the hero):**

> Solutions Advisory Partners helps law, accounting, and consulting firms automate the manual work that eats their margins: intake, drafting, billing, client reporting. Every automation is proven against written success criteria before it scales. If it doesn't pass, you stop, keep everything, and know exactly why.

**The narrative bridge (kept from the Proofworks draft, retargeted).** A Solutions Engineer's job is discovery, solution mapping, proof on the buyer's own data, and a clean handoff. That is, word for word, the job of a good automation consultant. Ten years of enterprise POCs is ten years of being professionally punished for over-promising, which is exactly the instinct missing from the AI consulting market.

For the professional-services owner, the bridge lands differently than it would for a SaaS buyer. The message is not the SE career itself. It is what the career implies: *"I've spent a decade proving technology to skeptical enterprise buyers before they paid for it. I'll hold my own work to the same standard for you."*

**Proof points, in firing order (revised for this buyer):**

1. **The proof-first method with a written pass/fail gate.** The differentiator. Competitors' economics depend on never reaching a pass/fail moment.
2. **Practicing leader, not career consultant.** Currently Head of Solutions Engineering at Gen Digital. Named.
3. **Builder, not deck-maker, and no lock-in.** The client owns documented systems that survive after the engagement ends.
4. **Translator.** Explains AI in operator terms. This buyer's number one fear is being baffled and then billed.
5. **A decade of enterprise evaluations.** Background texture, not the lead. Enterprise logos mean less to a firm owner than seeing their own intake process automated.

**Honesty rules carried forward from the v1 credibility cleanup:** no invented logos or metrics, scenarios labeled illustrative, first-person singular voice. Boutique and senior is a feature for this ICP.

## 3. ICP

**Beachhead:** US professional-services firms — law, accounting/tax, consulting, financial advisory. Roughly 5–50 employees, ~$1M–$20M revenue, owner- or partner-led, no in-house technical staff. Buyer is the managing partner, owner, or practice manager/COO.

**Pains to name on the site, in their vocabulary:** client intake and onboarding, engagement letters and proposals, document drafting and review prep, billing and WIP tracking, client status reporting, email triage, scheduling and follow-ups.

**Disqualifiers:** firms already building AI with in-house technical staff; solo practitioners under ~$500k (can't fund the ladder); work requiring regulated-data handling beyond stated scope. The site should address data handling head-on in the FAQ rather than dodge it — this buyer will ask.

**Expansion (later, not on the site now):** other ops-heavy SMBs (agencies, specialty healthcare admin, distribution). Do not split focus in the first six months.

## 4. Offer ladder

| Offer | Scope | Price | Job |
|-------|-------|-------|-----|
| Readiness Assessment | 10-question quiz, on-site, instant score | Free | Lead capture, conversation starter |
| Automation Audit | 2–3 weeks. Inventory the manual work, score effort vs. impact, attach dollar values. Output: a ranked roadmap the firm owns. | $2.5k–$4k fixed | Paid discovery. The roadmap sells the next step. |
| Implementation | One automation built against **written success criteria** (hours saved, turnaround, error rate), on the firm's real data in the firm's real tools. Pass → scale. Miss → the firm stops, keeps everything, knows why. | $6k–$12k fixed, audit fee creditable | The flagship. The kill-switch is the brand promise. |
| Advisory / Enablement | Monthly guidance: the firm's own people (or their tools) build with SAP oversight. | $1.5k–$3k/mo, 3-month minimum | Recurring revenue that fits a 5–10 hr/wk practice. |

**Sequencing rule (put this on the site, plainly):** one paid engagement at a time. "I take one implementation at a time so yours gets finished" turns the capacity constraint into a scarcity signal, and it happens to be true.

**Pricing display:** show ranges on the site. This buyer distrusts "call for pricing."

Note vs. the Proofworks pricing ($4.5k–$7.5k / $8k–$20k / $3k–$10k/mo): those numbers were set for funded SaaS. The revised bands fit a $3M firm's budget and a 5–10 hr/wk delivery reality.

## 5. The method (kept, no longer name-bound)

Keep the four-move structure from the Proofworks draft. It was never dependent on the Proofworks name. Suggested labels under SAP: **Map → Build → Prove → Compound.**

1. **Map** — the Automation Audit. Inventory, score, attach dollar values, rank.
2. **Build** — implementation against success criteria written down *first*.
3. **Prove** — read the scorecard. Pass → scale. Miss → stop, client keeps everything.
4. **Compound** — roll out, train, document; one new proven automation per cycle under advisory.

Use it everywhere: services page, proposals, LinkedIn content. It is structurally hard for hype-driven competitors to copy because their economics depend on never reaching a pass/fail moment.

## 6. GTM: the content engine

This is the **only** channel. Honest expectations, agreed in the ideation session: content funnels to trust-bought SMB buyers compound over 6–12 months. First revenue is realistically month 3–6 at the earliest, not day 45. The Proofworks day-45/day-90 revenue targets are void.

**Weekly budget:** of the 5–10 hours, reserve 2–3 for content and the rest for delivery and sales conversations. Content is the marketing department; it cannot be the thing that gets cut every week.

**Content pillars, niched to professional services:**

1. **Workflow teardowns** (primary): one specific firm process per post — intake, engagement letters, billing, client reporting — with before/after, hours and dollars, and honest caveats. This is how a stranger earns trust with this buyer.
2. **Buyer education:** how to evaluate an AI vendor, why AI pilots fail, what a success criterion looks like. (The Proofworks "POC-buyer's guide" article retargets cleanly here.)
3. **SE narrative** (occasional): the enterprise-proof story as personal credibility. Sparingly — it must never read as enterprise-speak to a firm owner.

**Cadence:** minimum 1 LinkedIn post/week, 1 site article/month. All content passes the SAP voice rules (no em dashes, no engagement-bait closers, no "in the age of AI," cut any sentence that could be sent to anyone).

**Assessment funnel:** the quiz is the site's single CTA. Rewrite it in professional-services vocabulary (see §7). Score email triggers a personal follow-up within 24 hours — this is the one place the funnel gets a human touch, so it cannot slip.

**LinkedIn profile:** update headline, publish a pinned SE→automation narrative post at launch.

**Deliberately out of the plan** (Nico's call, made with the tradeoff on the table): cold email, cold DMs, referral partners, in-person events, and the one-time warm-intro batch to the SaaS network. Checkpoint at day 90: if there have been zero sales conversations, the warm-intro batch is the first lever to revisit.
